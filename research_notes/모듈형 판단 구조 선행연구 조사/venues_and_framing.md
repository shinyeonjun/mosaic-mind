## 2. Archival international options: ACL Findings (via ARR), TMLR

### Takeaway
**TMLR** suits a rigorous small-scale study best. It explicitly accepts modest contributions as long as the claims are backed by evidence, and it has no conference deadline. **ARR** (ACL Rolling Review) gives a path to *ACL 2027 / Findings* through cycles ending in January 2027. A Korean-language synthetic study fits the NLP framing, but NLP reviewers will push for real-text validation.

### Cited Findings
- TMLR asks two questions: are the claims supported by "accurate, convincing and clear evidence", and would some of its audience be interested? Modest contributions are acceptable. Papers are rejected for unsupported bold claims or false novelty claims. Certifications include Featured, Reproducibility, and J2C (Journal-to-Conference) for presenting at NeurIPS, ICLR or ICML — [TMLR editorial policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- **ARR August 2026 cycle**: submissions opened Aug 3, 2026; reviews due Sep 7; author response Sep 14-24; meta-reviews Oct 8; cycle ends Oct 11, 2026 — [aiweekly summary](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/acl-rolling-review-opens-august-cycle-amid-nlp-submission-surge) (secondary source)
- One snippet gives Oct 6 as an October-cycle deadline, but this is unverified and may refer to a prior year — [aclweb portal](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/content/allow-early-submissions-acl-2026-october-arr-cycle)
- **EMNLP 2026** (Budapest) is closed: ARR deadline May 25, commitment Aug 2, notification Aug 20, 2026 — [zplatform](https://zplatform.ai/ai-event/emnlp-2026/) (aggregator)
- **ACL 2027** is in Kyoto, Aug 17-22, 2027. The last ARR route is the January 2027 cycle, and Findings is decided by the conference — [OpenCurious](https://www.opencurious.com/ai-conference-deadlines/acl-2027); [BERI](https://www.beri.net/events/acl-2027) (aggregators)

### Inferences
- Realistic archival route: an ICLR 2027 workshop (non-archival, for feedback), then TMLR or ARR Jan/Feb 2027 aimed at ACL 2027 (main or Findings).
- TMLR's criteria reward careful, well-scoped claims. That favors framing the paper as a controlled mechanism study, not as "beats LLMs".

## 4. What reviewers will require

### Takeaway
I found no single official checklist. Based on TMLR's criteria and the norms of NeurIPS/ICLR-style reproducibility checklists, the main weak points of this study are:
- 1-6 seeds
- the only LLM baseline is a single 1.2B model
- the data is synthetic only

Reviewers will push on each of these.

### Cited Findings
- TMLR rejects unsupported bold claims and false novelty claims, and requires accurate, convincing evidence — [TMLR editorial policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)

### Inferences (practitioner norms, not sourced in this session)
- **Seeds and statistics**: use at least 5 seeds (ideally 10) for every headline comparison. Report mean ± 95% CI or bootstrap CIs, and run paired tests across seeds. A cheap 8GB GPU setup makes this affordable for small judges. Where only 1-3 seeds exist, label the result explicitly as preliminary.
- **Baselines**:
  - A stronger LLM. One option is a 7-8B model in 4-bit with QLoRA, which can fit in 8GB at small sequence lengths. Another is few-shot prompting of an open 7-8B instruct model.
  - Standard compositional-generalization baselines: a Transformer trained from scratch, and data augmentation or meta-seq2seq-style methods.
  - For the equivariance claim, an established invariant/equivariant set or permutation model (DeepSets, Set Transformer, or an equivariant GNN) and a non-equivariant judge of the same size. The non-equivariant judge isolates the inductive bias, which the study partly already does.
- **Second domain or real text**: synthetic-only Korean rule worlds will draw a "toy" criticism. Add at least one external benchmark (for example SCAN, COGS, CLUTRR, or a Korean NLI subset). Alternatively, give a clear argument for why the controlled synthetic setting is the point of the study.
- **Related work**: compositional generalization benchmarks, neural module networks, equivariant and symmetry-based learning, emergent communication (claim 3), and Bayesian/meta-learned inference for source trust (claim 4).
- **Reproducibility**: release code and data generators with configs. Report compute, which here is one 8GB GPU — a selling point for "accessible research".

## 5. arXiv preprint norms for first-time submitters (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Since **Jan 21, 2026**, an institutional email no longer auto-endorses a new arXiv author in any category. An individual researcher must get **personal endorsement from an established arXiv author** in cs.LG or cs.CL. Also, CS **survey/position papers** need prior peer review (since Oct 2025), and every submission must include a **full English version** (since Feb 11, 2026). Research papers like this one are not affected by the survey/position rule.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv requires endorsement before a first submission or a submission to a new category. Endorsers should be people you know or whose work you have reviewed, ideally in your field. You request endorsement with a code obtained when you start a submission — [arXiv endorsement help](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)
- As of Jan 21, 2026, institutional email addresses are no longer the sole qualifier for endorsement of new authors. This was piloted in Math on Dec 10, 2025 — [arXiv blog Math pilot](https://blog.arxiv.org/arxiv/2025/12/10/updated-endorsement-policy-for-arxiv-mathematics); [CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-2026-endorsement-english-ai-policy-changes), which cites blog.arxiv.org/2026/01/21/attention-authors-updated-endorsement-policy/
- CS surveys and position papers have required documented prior peer-review acceptance since Oct 2025 — [CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-2026-endorsement-english-ai-policy-changes)
- A full English version is required, effective Feb 11, 2026 (announced Nov 21, 2025); faithful machine translation is allowed — [CASRAI summary](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-2026-endorsement-english-ai-policy-changes)
- arXiv posted an updated rate-limit policy on Oct 1, 2026 — [arXiv blog 2026](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026)

### Inferences
- Practical path: post to arXiv after a workshop acceptance, or ask a Korean academic contact or a workshop co-author with arXiv history to endorse. A Korean-only KSC paper cannot go on arXiv without a full English version.

## 6. Framing and strengthening recommendations (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Frame the paper as a **controlled mechanism study**: "which inductive bias drives compositional generalization in modular reader+judge systems?" Do not frame it as "small models beat LLMs". Put claim (2) (equivariance, not interface format) at the center, and present claims (3) and (4) as supporting analyses or a separate short paper.

### Cited Findings
- TMLR values modest but well-supported claims and penalizes overclaiming — [TMLR editorial policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- A symmetry/equivariance-focused workshop (NeurReps) and a compositional-learning workshop series (ICML 2026) exist — [GitHub list](https://github.com/YLfan99/neurips2026-workshops); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54096)

### Inferences
- Suggested timeline:
  1. KSC 2026 (Korean short paper, if the deadline is still open) → feedback.
  2. Strengthen the paper (seeds ≥5, a 7-8B 4-bit baseline, an equivariant baseline, one external benchmark).
  3. ICLR 2027 workshop (~Feb 2027).
  4. arXiv (with endorsement).
  5. TMLR or ARR → ACL 2027 / Findings, or NeSy 2027.
- Consider splitting the work. Claims 1-2 (compositional generalization plus the equivariance ablation) make one paper. Claims 3-4 (emergent anchored messages; meta-learned Bayes-approaching trust) could go to a meta-learning or emergent-communication venue.
- Present "one 8GB GPU" as accessible, reproducible science and add a compute statement.
